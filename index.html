Introduction

Building under constraints became its own education.

Most of my work began without formal infrastructure, institutional access, or funding. What started as curiosity gradually became a pattern: identifying difficult real-world problems, teaching myself the tools required to approach them, and building systems around them anyway.

My projects span healthcare machine learning, environmental systems, scientific writing, education access, astronomy, and engineering.

“How do we make advanced knowledge and technology more accessible to the people who are usually excluded from it?”

How I Think

Interdisciplinary thinking is not optional anymore.

I’m especially interested in problems where technical systems interact with human constraints: healthcare accessibility, unequal educational infrastructure, low-resource environments, and information asymmetry.

A recurring theme across my work is reducing the distance between advanced systems and the people who normally cannot access them.

I don’t see disciplines as isolated categories. To me, healthcare, education, language, environmental systems, and engineering are all connected through one thing: how humans organize knowledge and opportunity.

Most of what I know was self-taught out of necessity. That process forced me to adapt quickly, think independently, and build without waiting for ideal conditions.
