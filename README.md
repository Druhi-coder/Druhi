# Druhi Sarupria

Independent researcher, builder, educator, and writer from Rajasthan, India.

This repository contains the source code for my personal portfolio website — a space documenting my work across healthcare AI, education accessibility, sustainability, scientific communication, and interdisciplinary systems building.

---

## About

Most of my work began without formal infrastructure, institutional access, or funding.

What started as curiosity gradually became a pattern:
- identifying difficult real-world problems,
- teaching myself the tools required to approach them,
- and building systems around them anyway.

A recurring theme across my work is reducing the distance between advanced knowledge, technology, and the people who are usually excluded from it.

## Education & Accessibility — DRiSe Foundation

Founder of **DRiSe Foundation**, a community-led initiative focused on:
- foundational education,
- STEM accessibility,
- digital literacy,
- and AI awareness for underserved women and children.

Through outreach initiatives, workshops, and self-authored educational material, over **5,000 women and underprivileged children** have been reached across underserved communities.

---

## Sustainability — Dhara Tattva

Building **Dhara Tattva**, a circular-economy initiative exploring low-cost environmental systems that convert organic waste into usable products including:
- incense sticks,
- manure,
- and recycled paper.

The initiative has contributed to the management of:
- **32+ tons of organic waste**
- **27.48 tons of food waste**

Research work has also received IIT Delhi-supported evaluation and recognition.

# Philosophy

I care less about building impressive-looking systems and more about building systems that remain useful under real-world constraints.

That often means:
- interpretability over hype,
- accessibility over exclusivity,
- and long-term usefulness over short-term attention.

I don’t see disciplines as isolated categories.

To me, healthcare, education, environmental systems, engineering, language, and scientific communication are all connected through one question:

> How do we make advanced knowledge and opportunity more accessible to the people who normally cannot access them?
